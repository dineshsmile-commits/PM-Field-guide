Team PM Field Guide The big sip RBI tightens cyber oversight of non-bank payment system operators Business Standard · 5 November 2026 What happened: On 5 November, RBI issued consolidated Master Directions on Cyber Resilience and Digital Payment Security Controls for non-bank payment system operators, including NPCI, card networks, payment aggregators and wallet issuers. Large and medium operators must comply now; small ones have until 1 April 2028. Operators need source code (or escrow, or a fresh developer certificate) for critical third-party apps, must report incidents within six hours, and keep a 12-hour cooling-off after a customer changes a registered mobile number or email. Why it matters: Rules like these land directly in the product: onboarding, account recovery, vendor choice and release cadence. One rulebook replacing six older circulars also makes compliance easier to reason about. If you were the PM: Map every flow that touches a number or email change, and decide how to explain the 12-hour wait so it feels like protection. List your critical vendor apps and check the source-code position for each, since that shapes your release process. Then ask what six-hour incident reporting looks like at 2 am. Framework of the fortnight Kano model, applied to ChatGPT's Wallet Kano sorts features into basics users expect, performance features where more is better, and delighters nobody asked for. In ChatGPT's Wallet, an agent that buys for you is the delighter. The review screen, biometric approval and payment token are basics: nobody praises them, but one failure breaks trust. OpenAI plans spending limits and category controls later. Kano suggests those will soon be basics too, so they deserve priority over more delight. Explore Kano and the other frameworks . Something to chew on "You are the PM for a payment app. RBI's rules require a 12-hour cooling-off period before online payments after a user changes their registered mobile number or email. How would you implement it without losing users?" A strong answer covers: The user and the risk: who changes numbers, and how the wait blocks account takeover. The experience: warn before the change, show a countdown, and say what still works during the wait. The metrics: drop-off and support tickets against fraud losses, with edge cases checked with compliance.
