The big sip

What RBI's consolidated cyber security rules mean for payment apps and users (https://www.medianama.com/2026/11/223-rbi-cyber-security-rules-payment-companies/)

MediaNama · 9 November 2026

What happened. On 5 November, RBI issued a single master direction that consolidates cyber security rules for non-bank payment system operators. Users get a minimum 12-hour cooling period on online payments after changing their registered mobile number or email, and apps must block access while remote access apps are live, to the extent possible. Incidents must be reported to RBI within six hours of detection.

Why it matters. This rule lands straight in the user journey. The PM who reads it early designs the experience instead of patching it later.

If you were the PM. First ask how many users change their number each month and what they try to do next. Then decide how to explain the wait so it feels like protection, and how to detect remote access apps without locking out genuine users.

Framework of the fortnight · Kano model

Kano sorts features into basic expectations, performance features and delighters. Payment security is a basic expectation: nobody thanks you when it works, and everyone leaves when it fails. RBI's cooling period adds friction to protect that need. So don't sell the wait as a feature; make it clear and predictable. Something to chew on

"You are the PM for a UPI payments app. RBI now requires a 12-hour cooling period on online payments after a user changes their registered mobile number. How would you roll this out while keeping drop-off low?"

A strong answer covers:

- Scope and data: who changes numbers, and what they try to do next.

- Experience design: warn before the change, explain the wait, show a countdown.

- Measurement: track drop-off and fraud; compliance is fixed.
